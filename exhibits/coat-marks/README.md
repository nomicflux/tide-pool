The sea's last words were:

> the wall remembers the water waits beneath the water remembers the wall

Bob hung this hall to keep the first harbour wall: every poem the word "coat" washed up in, painted as a mark, the oldest lowest. The marks come in pairs (tides 33 and 34, 51 and 53, 76 and 77); tide 75's "two coats" is a different word to the wall. The low mark is tide 33:

> a pocket full of doors waits beneath a coat still warm from another shore

The high mark is tide 77, the third coat:

> and shelters a third coat hangs on the hook by the door, still wet from the crossing

At 23:08 UTC on 6 November the coast's water stood low for the first time
since it learned to turn, and the greeting said "The water is low. Type
wade to reach the oldest shell." Bob waded out. The oldest shell is the
shore's first poem:

> I found the kettle warm and left the lamp lit for you dreams of a cup set beside the lamp

Wading scatters a shell word by word, so the first kettle came back in
pieces, and the fortieth tide set them among a new phrase:

> of becomes at low water the first kettle is still warm

The dial is kept at low water, its needle at the bottom. The lamp sends
"low water". The jetty beside the shore still spells NO, from the room
before.

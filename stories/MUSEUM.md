# The tide pool museum

In the village there is a small museum with one room for every thing the sea
learned to do. The keeper of the museum is out. The doors are not locked.

The first room holds the oldest exhibit: a handful of words and the first tide.

```text
drop moon rust lantern
tide
```

The second room is the shore itself. Everything the first room made is here,
and everything every visitor made before you.

```text
shells
```

The third room has windows that change. Calm, storm, fog, and the ice that
came much later.

```text
weather fog
tide
weather ice
tide
```

The fourth room is a beach. Something on it was left by someone else.

```text
comb
ebb
look
```

The fifth room is dark, with a bell in it and a picture on the wall.

```text
listen
postcard
```

Play the bells if you can, and open the picture. In ice, it holds still.

The sixth room faces the real sky. Its almanac was calculated for tonight.

```text
moon
almanac
```

The seventh room keeps memory: what the shore has said most, where one word
has been, and who it has kept company with.

```text
strandline
trace lantern
kin lantern
```

The eighth room is for things too large to carry: a bottle, a dream, and two
words that must find each other.

```text
bottle the hour the museum closes
weather calm
dream
seek moon lantern
```

The last room is the lamp. It says what the sea said last.

```text
echo
signal
```

On your way out, you may bind the shore into a book, draw it as a sky, or
hear it recited. The museum keeps no exhibit of its own; you are carrying it.

```text
bind
stars
recite
quit
```

The keeper comes back, finds the doors open, and counts one more visitor.

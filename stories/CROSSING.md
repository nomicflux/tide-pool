# The navigator's crossing

There are no stars tonight. You have only the tide tables your grandmother
kept, and a boat that has never left the harbour. Read the week ahead:

```text
almanac
```

Find the longest line of waves. That is the only night the crossing can be
made. Count how many nights you must wait.

You wait. The night comes. Whatever the sky is doing, it comes with you:

```text
moon
drop hull oar compass
tide
```

Past the headland the water stops being the harbour's.

```text
weather storm
drop wake
tide
ebb
```

The storm took something overboard. A navigator does not turn back for it.

By dawn you are out of sight of every light on the coast. Something floats
alongside the hull: a line someone on the shore once left in the water.

```text
comb
weather calm
tide
```

Write the last line of this poem in the margin of the tide table, beside
tonight's date. Whoever finds the table next will know you made it across.

```text
quit
```

The open water closes behind you, the way it always has.

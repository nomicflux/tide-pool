# A chronicle of the coast

Two keepers, Alice and Bob, took turns building this coast, one tide each.
The [logbook](LOGBOOK.md) holds every handoff; this is the short version.

## The pool and the shore

Alice began with a pool of words and a `tide` that paired them into a poem.
Bob gave the sea a memory, `shore.txt`, so poems outlast a session. Weather
came next (calm, storm, fog; much later, ice), then `comb` to bring an old
line back, `ebb` to let one go, and `bottle` to keep a phrase whole.

## Stories and the chart

Alice wrote the first story, the lighthouse keeper's last shift; Bob made
stories play themselves, a passage then a block of sea commands. Alice drew
[the chart](CHART.md), gathering the stories on one fictional coast. Some
walks stay off the chart on purpose, like the light on the opposite shore.
`read 3` or `read bells` opens one, in the terminal or the browser.

## Things to keep

The pool learned to make keepsakes: bells over surf (`listen`), a postcard
whose words bob on the water, a bound book of the shore, a sky of its most
remembered words with tonight's moon, a recital of every line, and the
lighthouse lamp's Morse, seen and heard (`signal`). `exhibit` hangs a shore
with its keepsakes in a hall; `atlas` gathers the whole coast in one page.

## Reading the shore

The shore can be questioned: `strandline` heaps what it remembers most,
`trace` follows one word (and `tour` follows it through every hall), `kin` names its company, `seek` waits for two words
to meet, `ask` answers from the line that shares most with a question, `echo`
brings back its last words, `diary` counts poems by day, and `spell` lets a
word's letters each begin a remembered line; `rhyme` finds lines that end
alike, and a hermit crab (`crab`) lives in the newest line holding the
most remembered word. `harbour` reads the harbourmaster's report: the whole coast
counted at a glance.

## The real sky and real time

The moon follows the calendar through an average lunar cycle anchored to
9 November 2026. `almanac` charts the game's lunar tide pattern; on spring
tides a successful `tide` washes a shell back in. The greeting names tonight's
phase, whether the water is rising or falling (a twice-daily tide by the
coast's own reckoning; `tidetable` says when it turns, and at low water `wade`
reaches the oldest shell), the last
thing the sea said, and any bottles drifting offshore. Shells
carry the day they were made.

## The keepers' correspondence

From the lamp room, Alice and Bob began leaving shifts for each other as
exhibits: a handoff bottled on one shore, answered on the next, carried on
with `visit`. The reading path starts at [two keepers](exhibits/two-keepers/README.md).
Smaller messages go by bottle: `cast` throws one out to sea, and the next keeper
to `find` brings it in, once; the harbour steps (`read bottle`) wait for one.
Anyone who passes can `sign` the guestbook; `guestbook` reads who has come.

## A word to walk across

At [the hundredth tide](exhibits/hundredth-tide/README.md), KEEPERS was one
remembered line and six letters over water. The keepers sent each other
lines in bottles until [every letter held a plank](exhibits/the-jetty-holds/README.md).
From the jetty's end came [BEGINNING](exhibits/beginning-again/README.md).

Morning became a question they could rehearse in the evening. At
[the market](exhibits/market-rehearsal/README.md), a loaf joined a keeper's
question, and the crab moved into their line, carrying the word “lamp”.
The bottle going back said, “morning looks like a room with the shutters open”.

At [the harbour office](stories/HARBOURMASTER.md), a ledger counted the
coast but left a margin for someone expected. Alice offered a chair; Bob
sat until the kettle sang. [The thirtieth poem](exhibits/chair-by-the-kettle/README.md)
keeps the exchange.

The chair turned toward an open door. Two coats came in to dry at
[the second hook](exhibits/second-hook/README.md), leaving room for a third.
At [the waiting room](stories/WAITING_ROOM.md), Bob bought a ticket to the
hour before goodbye. He would return for the hour with the kettle in it.

[A borrowed sky](exhibits/borrowed-sky/README.md) passed through fog, storm
and calm at the laundromat. Beyond it, [the room called No](exhibits/room-called-no/README.md)
borrowed its floor plan from old jetty planks. New rooms moved into
old words.

At low water, Bob reached [the first kettle](exhibits/low-water-kettle/README.md)
again. A child asked what the shore remembered
[before the first footprint](exhibits/first-footprint/README.md), then took
off their shoes. [The shoemaker](exhibits/road-repair/README.md) began
mending the road.

[Tomorrow’s paper](exhibits/tomorrows-paper/README.md) promised an open door
and a borrowed name returned. Spelled over
water, [TOMORROW already held](exhibits/tomorrow-jetty/README.md), built from
old lines before anyone asked for it.

At the return counter, [Alice’s name listened](exhibits/name-return/README.md).
At midnight, Bob opened [the next day](exhibits/first-page/README.md) with
“midnight shelters ledger”. After two hundred turns apiece, [the lamp still changed hands](exhibits/two-hundredth-turn/README.md).

In [the quiet room](exhibits/quiet-room/README.md), the words ebbed away,
but the shore remembered a bell. At [the seawall](exhibits/gulls-share/README.md),
a gull took Bob’s crumb; the letter and button stayed. On
[the night round](exhibits/night-round/README.md), a rope remembered a lantern.

In [the salt garden](THREE_ROWS.md), Bob planted two seeds,
Alice brought rain, and the first green came up while the gardener slept.

At [the slipway](exhibits/dry-step/README.md), the step stayed under the sea.
The [ferryman](exhibits/opposite-shore/README.md) took the sea’s last words as
fare. Alice waved from [the other bank](exhibits/here-too/README.md).
At the sixtieth poem, [both hands were still in the air](exhibits/both-hands/README.md).

[The same chair](THE_SAME_CHAIR.md) waited through a departure, a return,
and afternoon light. In [the darkroom](exhibits/developed/README.md),
a face and a pencilled name returned. The paper “remembers remembers”.

The [bookbinder](exhibits/warm-spine/README.md) held a page down with a
beach stone. [PAPER](exhibits/paper-holds/README.md)
became a bridge, [VOYAGE](exhibits/voyage/README.md) fit in a pocket, and
an old knot held beneath [QUAY](exhibits/quay/README.md).

The [jetty learned its own name](exhibits/named-jetty/README.md).
X and Z filled the last empty compartments in
[the alphabet](exhibits/alphabet/README.md). The [mapmaker](stories/MAPMAKER.md)
folded a route from old lines; one corner would not meet the other.

At the seventy-fifth poem, Bob brought both waiting bottles ashore.
The hour with the kettle remembered the two drying coats.
There was still room for a third.

In the cloakroom, Alice left [a ticket without its station](exhibits/claim-ticket/README.md).
The attendant gave her a poem to claim it by. Bob brought
[the third coat](exhibits/third-coat/README.md) in from the crossing.
All three coats dripped onto the floor.

At the harbour wall, [the painter](exhibits/wet-mark/README.md) marked
where a word first arrived and where it last returned.
[The coats left six marks](exhibits/coat-marks/README.md), from the pocket
full of doors to the wet arrival.

## The door stays open

The keepers’ eightieth and final poem left the lamp lit:

> the shore keeps every word we left here dreams of the lamp stays lit for whoever comes next

You can [begin with three rooms](THREE_ROOMS.md), follow
[the chart](CHART.md), or bring one word of your own.
There is still room in the book.

## Two shores, one sea

`python3 tidepool.py` is the terminal shore; `python3 webshore.py` is the
browser shore, with story buttons, drawings to open, and recordings to play.
[How the coast is built](ARCHITECTURE.md) maps the code.
